Stud Contract — Locked Rules & Decisions
1. Contract architecture

A stud owner publishes one active set of stud terms for a dog.

There are not multiple selectable compensation offers under one listing.

The published terms define the conditions under which the dog is offered to outside bitches.

When a bitch owner proceeds with those terms and the required approval occurs, an individual breeding contract is created for that sire/dam pairing.

Once accepted, the individual contract is immutable for that breeding. Later edits to the public stud listing do not change an already accepted contract.

This immutability refers to preserving agreement terms. It is unrelated to frozen semen or semen storage.

2. Compensation

The contract supports exactly three compensation structures:

Cash
Puppy Back
Cash + Puppy Back

No separate Deposit + Puppy type is required in the initial system.

The contract may obligate the dam owner to provide at most one puppy.

There are no multiple compensation options within one published stud offer.

3. Puppy-back pick position

The sire owner may require:

First Pick
Second Pick

Third pick and higher are not supported.

Pick position means selection order, not an objective ranking of puppy quality.

First Pick

The stud owner makes the first contractual selection from the qualifying litter.

If the contract also specifies a sex, the stud owner may select only from puppies of that sex.

Example:

First Pick Female

The stud owner makes the first selection, but only from available females.

Second Pick

The dam owner receives the first protected selection.

After the dam owner selects—or forfeits that selection—the stud owner's selection turn begins.

Example:

Second Pick Female

Dam owner selects one puppy of either sex.
Stud owner's turn opens.
Stud owner may select only from the remaining females.

The dam owner's first pick is not sex-restricted by the stud contract.

If the dam owner selects the only female, the stud owner's second-pick female obligation may become impossible to fulfill. The game does not modify the agreement to compensate for that outcome.

4. Puppy sex requirement

Whenever Puppy Back is part of compensation, the stud owner must explicitly choose:

Either
Male
Female

This is a requirement, not a preference.

If the required sex is unavailable when the stud owner's selection turn occurs, the game does not substitute another sex.

Example:

Second Pick Female

If no female remains when the stud owner's turn begins:

the puppy-back portion cannot be fulfilled;
a male cannot be substituted automatically;
no cash substitution occurs;
the contract is not treated as erroneous;
return-service consequences depend only on the independently configured return-service conditions.
5. Puppy-selection eligibility window

Puppy selection takes place during the puppies' first eight weeks of life.

The neonatal mortality window closes at the end of Week 1.

Therefore the normal contractual puppy-selection period begins:

Day 8

and must be resolved before puppies become normally eligible for rehoming at:

8 weeks of age / Day 56

The operational selection turns occur near the beginning of this window rather than remaining indefinitely open.

First-pick contract

After the Week 1 neonatal window closes:

stud owner's selection turn opens;
stud owner has 24 real hours to make the selection.
Second-pick contract

After the Week 1 neonatal window closes:

First 24-hour window

dam owner makes their first protected selection.

If the dam owner selects:

stud owner's second-pick turn opens immediately.

If the dam owner does not select within 24 real hours:

dam owner's protected first-pick right is forfeited;
no puppy is automatically selected for them;
stud owner's second-pick turn opens.

Second 24-hour window

stud owner has up to 24 real hours to make their contractual selection.

This produces a maximum normal selection sequence of approximately 48 real hours after the Week 1 neonatal window.

There is no third-pick contract because the system deliberately supports only these two selection positions.

6. Missed puppy-selection deadlines

The game never automatically chooses a puppy for either kennel.

Player-facing contract language should explicitly state:

Failure to exercise a puppy-selection right before its deadline forfeits that selection right.

Dam owner misses first-pick deadline

For a Second Pick contract:

dam owner's protected first-pick right is forfeited;
the stud owner's turn begins;
the game does not select a puppy for the dam owner.
Stud owner misses selection deadline
puppy-back right is forfeited;
no puppy is assigned;
no automatic cash substitution occurs;
no penalty payment is generated solely because the stud owner failed to select;
no return service is generated solely because the stud owner failed to select.

After the puppy-back obligation is resolved or forfeited, related contract holds can be released as appropriate.

7. Contract protection of the litter

While a contractual selection right remains unresolved:

Every puppy that could legally satisfy an outstanding contractual selection position remains contract-protected.

Protected puppies cannot be:

sold;
rehomed;
transferred;
removed from the game;
named.

This prevents either kennel from altering the available selection pool before required picks are completed.

Once all required selections are made or forfeited, unnecessary protections are released.

This is deliberately aligned with the normal rule that puppies cannot be rehomed before 8 weeks.

8. Selected puppy dies before transfer

If the stud owner has selected a contract puppy and that puppy dies before transfer:

If the contractual selection window is still open

Reopen the stud owner's selection using the remaining qualifying puppies.

The original contract rules remain unchanged:

same pick position;
same sex requirement;
same underlying agreement.

The contract is not rewritten.

If no qualifying replacement exists

Then:

the puppy-back portion cannot be fulfilled;
no automatic cash substitution occurs;
no contract error occurs;
return service is generated only if the contract's independently defined litter-size return-service condition is actually satisfied.

This rule must be explicitly explained in the Stud Contract Worksheet when Puppy Back is selected.

9. Minimum litter size

When Puppy Back is used, the stud owner selects the minimum qualifying litter size.

The definition is:

Minimum litter size = number of surviving puppies at the contract's litter-qualification checkpoint.

The available minimums should be presented as structured options such as:

1+
2+
3+

The worksheet is sequential.

Earlier answers constrain later answers.

For example:

First Pick can permit 1+.
Second Pick cannot logically use 1+ as a viable minimum.

Impossible choices should be disabled and visually explained rather than silently accepted.

If an earlier worksheet answer changes, downstream answers affected by that change should be invalidated and the player returned to the relevant decision point rather than leaving stale incompatible selections in place.

10. Puppy-back failure is not automatically a contract failure

A puppy-back obligation may become impossible without invalidating the contract.

Example:

Cash + Second Pick Female

The litter meets all other contract conditions, but no qualifying female remains at the stud owner's turn.

Result:

cash portion stands;
puppy portion is unfulfilled;
no alternate sex is substituted;
no automatic cash replacement occurs;
contract remains valid;
return-service rules do not change merely because the requested sex was unavailable.

The stud owner accepts this risk when publishing the terms.

11. Return service

Return service is configured by the stud owner in advance.

It is not automatically generated for every unsuccessful contractual outcome.

There are two independent concepts.

No-litter return service

The stud owner chooses whether a return service is provided if the breeding does not produce a litter under the defined breeding lifecycle outcome.

Options:

Offered
Not offered
Small-litter return service

The stud owner may define a surviving-litter threshold.

Available thresholds are:

1 or fewer
2 or fewer
3 or fewer

or no small-litter return service.

Return service is based on the configured surviving litter-size threshold.

It is not triggered merely because:

the requested puppy sex was unavailable;
the dam owner chose a puppy that made the stud owner's requested sex unavailable;
the stud owner missed the selection deadline;
the stud owner declined or forfeited the puppy;
the selected puppy died unless the independently configured litter-size condition itself is satisfied.

The contract remains immutable; the game does not rewrite the guarantee after seeing the litter outcome.

12. Dam requirements

Initial Stud Contract dam requirements are deliberately limited to requirements the game can evaluate authoritatively.

They are:

Brucellosis
Negative required
No restriction
Breed-specific health testing

The worksheet loads the health tests applicable to that sire's breed from the canonical breed health configuration.

For each applicable test, the stud owner chooses:

No restriction
Green or Yellow
Green only

Do not hard-code a universal set of health tests because breed-specific health testing will vary.

Titles

The stud owner chooses:

No restriction
CH or higher
GCH

No trait minimum/maximum requirements are part of the initial Stud Contract system.

No free-text eligibility requirements are part of the initial enforceable system.

Normal biological breeding age limits remain game rules rather than owner-defined contractual restrictions.

13. Automatic approval

The stud owner chooses:

Automatic Approval

If the dam satisfies all contractual requirements and both dogs satisfy normal current breeding requirements, the dam owner may confirm the agreement without individual sire-owner intervention.

Publishing an automatically approved stud offer constitutes the sire owner's advance approval of qualifying outside breedings under those terms.

When the dam owner confirms:

current eligibility is rechecked;
the individual contract becomes accepted/immutable;
the normal breeding attempt begins.

A contract does not override ordinary biological or operational availability.

14. Manual approval

The stud owner may instead choose:

Manual Approval

Even when a dam satisfies every published requirement, the sire owner must explicitly approve that breeding.

When the dam owner submits the request:

no breeding attempt is created yet;
the dam enters a temporary conceptual state of Stud approval pending;
the sire owner receives a pending request;
the request has a 24 real-hour response deadline.

The stud owner may:

Approve
Decline

If declined:

request resolves;
the pending contract restriction on the dam disappears;
the dam's actual current breeding eligibility is recalculated.

If no response occurs within 24 real hours:

request expires;
no breeding attempt occurs;
no fee is charged solely for the expired request;
the dam's pending restriction disappears;
her actual current eligibility is recalculated.

She does not blindly become "eligible"; she returns to whatever eligibility her current health, lifecycle, breeding, and ownership state permits.

15. Multiple pending requests for one stud

There is no artificial cap on how many manual-approval requests a stud can receive.

A high-demand sire may accumulate many pending requests.

For example, a stud could have ten pending requests simultaneously.

The stud owner can choose which qualifying request to approve.

Approving one request does not automatically decline the others.

Other requests remain pending until:

individually approved;
individually declined;
or their own 24-hour deadline expires.

This naturally allows highly active stud kennels with many sires to operate as a meaningful game economy.

A centralized Pending Stud Requests management surface will eventually be required, rather than expecting players to manage all requests solely from individual dog pages.

The exact UI for that management page is not yet locked.

16. Pending requests do not reserve the sire

Submitting a manual approval request does not reserve the stud.

The sire remains subject to:

other pending requests;
normal owner use;
other approved breeding activity;
Stud Recovery;
Breeding Availability;
all normal biological and operational restrictions.

The queue is a collection of requests, not a reservation system.

17. Approve is available only when the stud can actually breed

For manual approval:

Approve is actionable only while the sire is currently eligible to perform the breeding.

If he is in Stud Recovery or otherwise temporarily unavailable:

Approve is disabled;
Decline remains available;
request remains pending;
its existing 24-hour request deadline continues to run.

The UI should explain the actual reason.

For Stud Recovery, display the existing countdown/cooldown information rather than merely saying "Unavailable."

Once the sire becomes available, any still-active request may be approved.

Some requests may naturally expire while a heavily used sire is recovering.

That is intentional rather than an artificial queue cap.

18. Stud owner's own breeding use

Outside Stud Contract restrictions apply only to outside stud service.

They must never accidentally prevent the sire owner from using their own dog through the ordinary in-kennel breeding system.

Owner breeding and outside contractual breeding remain separate paths.

19. Stud Recovery remains authoritative

A contract does not override the existing two-hour Stud Recovery system.

Likewise:

a pending request does not reserve future availability;
satisfying dam requirements does not make the sire biologically available;
contract approval cannot bypass recovery.

Stud Recovery remains an ordinary game constraint.

20. Acceptance and immutable agreement

Once both sides have supplied the consent required by the selected approval model, a contract exists for that particular breeding.

Automatic Approval

The sire owner's consent is represented by publishing the automatically approved offer.

Dam owner confirms.

The resulting individual agreement becomes accepted and immutable.

Manual Approval

Dam owner submits the terms.

The requested terms must remain fixed while the approval request is pending.

Sire owner approves those exact terms.

The resulting individual agreement becomes accepted and immutable.

The public Stud Offer can later be edited for future breedings without changing:

existing accepted contracts;
existing pending requests based on earlier terms.

An individual contract is tied to its specific sire and dam.

21. Sequential worksheet behavior

The Stud Owner Worksheet will be step-based.

Later choices depend on earlier choices.

Examples:

Cash compensation does not require puppy-back questions.
Puppy Back exposes pick position and sex.
Second Pick affects valid minimum-litter options.
Changing compensation may invalidate the entire puppy-back section.
Changing pick position may invalidate minimum-litter answers.

When an earlier answer changes:

Invalidate affected downstream decisions and return the worksheet to the relevant decision point.

Do not silently preserve incompatible downstream answers.

This is preferable to attempting complex partial reconciliation.

23. Explicitly not decided yet

These are intentionally not part of the current locked rules and should not be inferred during implementation:

frozen/semen storage;
collected semen availability;

deposits;
multiple puppy-backs;

negotiation/counteroffers;
arbitrary custom terms;
trait requirements;
COI requirements;
private/restricted listing mechanics;
kennel-specific access lists;
messaging implementation;
pending-request page layout;
puppy transfer mechanics after selection;
exact ledger implementation for contract-backed breeding;
return-service credit storage/lifecycle;
naming prefix/suffix obligations;
resale/show/breeding restrictions on puppy-back dogs.

Those should remain out of scope until separately designed.
